## CUBE correspondence and limits

The public CUBE-LIO presentation describes six cube-face mapping, lower polar distortion than spherical projection, IDW hole filling, IGM/high-response semi-dense selection, direct photometric reprojection, and support for non-repetitive/low-density LiDAR including Livox. Relevant pages are slides 9–11, 13, 15, 21–22, and 25 of the [CUBE-LIO presentation](https://www.docswell.com/s/scomup/59N8N9-2026-03-22-173102). The ICRA program entry is [here](https://ras.papercept.net/conferences/conferences/ICRA26/program/ICRA26_ContentListWeb_4.html).

The f306 source has the expected broad components: six planar charts, per-scan intensity/depth rasterization, IDW filling, an IGM image channel, response-based features, and a direct projected residual with an analytic chain Jacobian. This supports a structural claim that the implementation follows the stated CUBE design direction. It does **not** establish exact paper equivalence. The presentation does not publish enough pseudocode to match the chosen dominant-axis basis, nearest-range collision rule, face-edge rejection, depth-aware support masks, or numeric thresholds one-for-one. The cubemap is also a gnomonic-style six-chart projection; it is not an equal-solid-angle pixelization.

## Shield4 / Livox source-level assessment

The failure cause cannot be assigned from this commit’s source alone. There is, however, a concrete timing/deskew risk worth external review:

1. The Shield4 config selects `lidar_type: 1`, which maps to `LIVOX`, uses `/livox/lidar`, `/imu/data`, and `filter_rate: 3`.
2. `livoxHandler` builds geometry from every third message point, while the photo side channel iterates every raw point. The scan end is taken from the last accepted geometry point’s offset.
3. The non-Ouster sync path consumes IMUs only up to the geometry-derived scan end. `PhotoObservation::prepare` deskews dense photo points only when their times lie inside the propagated history; outside that range it leaves the point at its uncorrected LiDAR/IMU-frame coordinate before rasterization. The inherited geometry path also has an uncorrected fallback for points after the history endpoint.

That code path can create a difference between the timestamps supporting geometry end-time/IMU history and the later points included in the photo image. It makes Livox timing/deskew adaptation a plausible contributor to Shield4 failure. It is not proof of causality: the exported source has no per-scan count of photo points outside propagated-history support, and this audit does not add instrumentation or rerun the bag. The P3 run outcomes remain in [`artifacts/p3/shield4_results.json`](../../artifacts/p3/shield4_results.json) and [`P3_FINAL_REPORT.md`](P3_FINAL_REPORT.md); they establish observed behavior, not the cause.

The code-level review should therefore answer two separate questions: whether the CUBE image/residual implementation is internally consistent, and whether Shield4’s Livox point-time support is sufficient for those image residuals. The latter remains unresolved until the fallback population and its effect are measured or otherwise independently established.
